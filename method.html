Method

Structured risk assessment discipline, developed in federal overseas security operations, applied to trade lanes, ports, and regulatory exposure.

Monitor

Tracking external developments that affect import operations, focused on ports, trade policy, and transportation.

Coverage is selective by design: signal over volume.

Assess

Signals are checked for credibility and relevance, separating real impact from background noise.

Likelihood and operational context are weighed before escalation.

Interpret

Developments are interpreted to clarify what actually changes, when it matters, and what it is likely to lead to.

The output is judgment and framing, not raw alerts.

Brief

Clients receive a weekly executive brief, with exception-based escalations when immediate attention is warranted.

The goal is confidence and focus for decision makers.

Coverage In Practice

Coverage is scoped to your exposure profile and operational sensitivity, not a fixed country list or constant alerting. The focus is on developments reasonably likely to affect your cost, timing, or execution.

Coverage Model

What I Monitor

Seven defined external risk domains.

  • Geopolitics & security Sanctions, conflict spillover, chokepoints, instability, and developments affecting routing and risk.
  • Trade policy, tariffs & enforcement Duties, quotas, regulatory shifts, CBP posture, exams, and compliance trends that alter landed cost or clearance risk.
  • Transportation & capacity Ocean carriers, blank sailings, routing shifts, rail and drayage constraints, intermodal bottlenecks, and service reliability.
  • Ports & terminals Congestion, dwell time, operational constraints, and local clearance friction.
  • Labor actions Contract risk, strikes, slowdowns, and union-led disruptions across ports and logistics networks.
  • Weather & natural disruption Severe weather events affecting ports, lanes, terminals, and inland networks.
  • Cyber & infrastructure risk System outages, ransomware, port IT disruption, and infrastructure vulnerabilities impacting logistics flow.

What You Receive

Structured, decision-ready output.

  • Exception-based escalation Urgent updates only when material changes are likely to imminently affect cost, timing, or execution. If nothing crosses that threshold, you receive the weekly brief.

  • Watchpoints What I'm watching next, and what would change the outlook.
  • Direct access by email Questions are welcome. I respond the same or next business day. I am not on call around the clock.

Common Questions

Why not just hire an analyst, rely on our broker, or use a data feed?

Hiring an analyst means headcount, ramp-up, and overhead. Brokers know their lanes, not yours. Dashboards generate alerts that still need someone to interpret them. IRI is the interpretation: independent, tailored, and without the cost of building a full risk function.

Isn't this just a list of curated headlines?

No. During onboarding I confirm your operating profile and set the initial watchlist and escalation triggers. After that, each brief is focused on what matters most for your operations.

What triggers an urgent alert, and how often will we hear from you?

Escalations are issued only when material changes are likely to imminently affect cost, timing, or execution for your lanes. If nothing crosses that threshold, you receive the weekly executive brief.

How do you translate risk into operational impact?

I translate developments into practical scenarios. What could change at the port or in policy, what it could do to lead times, inspections, capacity, or routing, and where costs can show up. I state confidence, what would confirm it, and what I am watching next.

What are your sources and how do you validate them?

I don't just forward links. I cross-check across multiple credible sources, favor primary or close-to-primary reporting, and look for confirmation in operational indicators. If something is unconfirmed, I label it that way and explain what would confirm or invalidate it.

What's out of scope?

I'm not inside your inventory, production, or supplier systems, so I'm not assessing internal supply chain risk. I'm also not on-call real-time crisis management. I focus on external drivers that change outcomes for imports and translate them into decision-relevant impact.

How fast can you respond to a direct question?

I'm email-based, but responsive. If you email a question, I typically reply the same business day or next business day, depending on complexity. If something is clearly time-sensitive, I'll move faster when I can.

What happens if nothing changes for weeks?

You still get a weekly executive brief. I'm checking the environment every day, even if I'm not running a 24/7 desk. If nothing meaningful changes, I'll say that, confirm what still looks stable, and flag what I'm watching next so you're not surprised later.

What do you need from us to get started?

A short intake on trade lanes, key ports, origin regions, product sensitivity, and decision constraints. Coverage is then tuned to what would actually move cost, timing, or execution.
