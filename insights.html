Insights

Examples of what an IRI alert or weekly brief includes: what changed, why it matters, who’s exposed, and what to watch next.

High-signal only. Not a newsfeed.

Jump to: Trade • Ports • Customs • Weather • Cyber

Trade

Example: Trade policy action with import cost exposure
Format mirrors an escalation note or brief item.

  • What changed: A policy signal suggests potential duty treatment changes or tighter enforcement focus.
  • Why it matters: Landed cost and clearance timelines can shift quickly once guidance and targeting posture solidify.
  • Who’s exposed: Importers with concentrated origin exposure, sensitive classifications, or thin-margin SKUs.
  • What to watch next: Effective dates, scope clarifications, enforcement language, and follow-on agency notices.
  • Bottom line: Plan for volatility until implementation details stabilize.

Ports & Logistics

Example: Port congestion or terminal friction that changes lead-time assumptions

  • What changed: Congestion indicators show deterioration (dwell, vessel bunching, appointments, rail flow, chassis availability).
  • Why it matters: Delays compound downstream (dray to warehouse receiving to customer service levels).
  • Who’s exposed: Importers using the gateway, time-sensitive inventory, intermodal moves, constrained dray capacity.
  • What to watch next: Whether congestion persists for multiple cycles, not just a single-day spike.
  • Bottom line: Treat early congestion as a lead-time multiplier, not a one-off delay.

Customs & Compliance

Example: Enforcement signal that predicts more holds or scrutiny

  • What changed: Official language indicates increased targeting or compliance emphasis on a product area or documentation topic.
  • Why it matters: More document requests, exams, and inconsistent release times can disrupt planning.
  • Who’s exposed: Importers in-scope, borderline classifications, weak supplier documentation, high broker override rates.
  • What to watch next: Follow-on notices, rulings, and broker-reported exam/hold patterns.
  • Bottom line: If you’re in-scope, tighten paperwork and classification discipline before you become the example.

Weather & Disruption

Example: Severe weather with logistics impacts beyond the headline

  • What changed: A weather system intersects with logistics nodes (ports, inland corridors, rail ramps, DC regions).
  • Why it matters: The recovery tail (staffing, closures, backlogs) often lasts longer than the event.
  • Who’s exposed: Moves transiting affected corridors, facilities in-region, tight delivery windows.
  • What to watch next: Escalation path (watch to warning), operational advisories, and recovery indicators.
  • Bottom line: Plan around the recovery lag, not the storm duration.

Cyber & Outage

Example: System outage at a node that silently slows cargo flow

  • What changed: A terminal, carrier, broker, or port community workflow degrades due to outage or cyber disruption.
  • Why it matters: Visibility drops, release workflows slow, and appointment queues build.
  • Who’s exposed: Importers dependent on the impacted operator, high-velocity turns, no redundancy in comms.
  • What to watch next: Scope confirmation, restoration timeline, and whether manual workarounds are effective.
  • Bottom line: When systems fail, time becomes the cost.
