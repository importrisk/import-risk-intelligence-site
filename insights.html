Escalation Alert
February 2026

Supreme Court Strikes Down IEEPA Tariffs — Section 122 15% Global Surcharge Effective February 24

Condition: Elevated
Coverage: All clients — U.S. imports (broad-based)
Impact: COST: HIGH • TIMING: LOW • EXECUTION: MODERATE
Following a Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariff authority, the administration issued a Presidential Proclamation imposing a temporary 15% global import surcharge under Section 122, effective February 24 for 150 days. Section 301 and Section 232 duties remain in effect.
▾
Alert issued

February 20, 2026: Alert distributed following publication of Supreme Court opinion and Presidential Proclamation.

Assessed impact

Cost: Most goods transition to a 15% Section 122 surcharge in addition to any applicable Section 301 or Section 232 duties. Immediate landed cost recalculation required.

Timing: No systemic clearance disruption expected, but implementation guidance and system updates may create short-term processing friction near the February 24 effective date.

Execution: Exemption categories (including USMCA goods, pharmaceuticals, critical minerals, and specified electronics) require verification. Unconfirmed SKUs may default to dutiable status pending documentation.

Decision window

Before February 24 effective date:

Validate exemption status at the SKU and HTS level. Refresh landed cost models using 15% Section 122 plus applicable Section 301/232 duties. Review duty exposure for in-transit shipments and evaluate refund eligibility for previously collected IEEPA duties. Confirm broker entry instructions align with updated surcharge codes prior to implementation.

Watchpoints

CBP implementation: CSMS messages, ACE updates, and documentation requirements tied to Section 122 entries beginning February 24.

Refund pathway: Treasury/CBP guidance or Court of International Trade filings defining procedure and timing for IEEPA duty recovery.

Primary sources
  1. Supreme Court: Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump — Opinion — Feb 20, 2026.
  2. White House: Presidential Proclamation — Section 122 temporary import surcharge — Effective Feb 24, 2026 (150 days).
  3. White House: Executive Order — Termination of certain IEEPA tariff actions
  4. CBP: CSMS Message — Ending collection of IEEPA duties / ACE guidance
Escalation Alert
January 2026

Major winter storm warnings issued across central U.S. corridors — elevated rail and intermodal disruption risk

Condition: Elevated Disruption Risk
Coverage: Auto parts importer — U.S. inland rail/intermodal via central corridors and key intermodal hubs
Impact: COST: LOW–MODERATE • TIMING: HIGH • EXECUTION: MODERATE–HIGH
National Weather Service winter storm warnings were issued across key Midwest corridors, with heavy snow and ice forecast to impact rail operations. Multiple railroads advised customers of expected service volatility as networks move into protective operating posture ahead of peak conditions.
▾
Assessed risk

Cost: Secondary costs may emerge if recovery causes bunching (detention, premium drayage, exception handling).

Timing: Expect potential transit variability during storm passage and initial recovery phase.

Execution: Railroads may implement speed restrictions, terminal protection measures, and irregular routings as conditions deteriorate.

Decision window

Next 48–96 hours: Pre-impact posture period.

Identify priority containers moving via affected corridors and authorize contingency options (alternate linehaul or premium drayage) if transit slips beyond acceptable thresholds. Convert fixed delivery commitments into buffered ETA ranges and prepare DC receiving teams for potential uneven arrivals during recovery.

Watchpoints

NWS updates: Upgrades from watch to warning and snowfall accumulation adjustments.

Rail advisories: Language referencing service impacts, embargoes, or extended transit times.

Recovery signals: “Network normalization” or “backlog reduction” language post-storm.

Trigger sources
  1. National Weather Service: Winter Storm Warnings issued across Midwest corridors — Jan 22, 2026.
  2. Norfolk Southern: Significant Winter Weather Alert — Jan 21, 2026.
  3. BNSF: Customer notification — major winter storm expected to impact network — Jan 22, 2026.
Escalation Alert
September 2025

Hong Kong Observatory raises Signal No. 8 — South China and Hong Kong terminals suspend operations ahead of Typhoon Ragasa

Condition: Critical
Coverage: Apparel importer — Asia exports via South China/Hong Kong gateways (vessel schedules, feeder connections, rollover risk)
Impact: COST: MODERATE • TIMING: HIGH • EXECUTION: HIGH
Following issuance of Hong Kong Tropical Cyclone Warning Signal No. 8, multiple South China and Hong Kong terminals suspended vessel and gate operations. Carriers began warning of schedule disruption, port call resequencing, and rollover risk as the storm approached peak intensity.
▾
Alert issued

September 22, 2025: Alert distributed following Signal No. 8 issuance and confirmation of terminal suspensions.

Assessed impact

Cost: Limited direct cost unless mode shift required; secondary cost risk emerges from rolled cargo and premium recovery services.

Timing: Vessel and feeder schedules likely to shift 2–5 days beyond closure window; transshipment connections vulnerable.

Execution: Elevated risk of omitted port calls, blank sailings, feeder disruption, and unstable downstream ETAs during restart phase.

Decision window

Next 48–96 hours:

Identify containers scheduled to load or transship via Hong Kong/South China during the storm window. Authorize carrier reroute or alternate port options where time sensitivity requires. Convert fixed ETA commitments to buffered ranges and prepare for potential feeder misalignment during recovery.

Watchpoints

Hong Kong Observatory: Downgrade of cyclone signal level.

Terminal operators: Confirmation of resumed gate/vessel operations.

Carrier advisories: Omitted calls, rolled bookings, and revised pro-forma schedules.

Primary trigger sources
  1. Hong Kong Observatory: Tropical Cyclone Warning Signal No. 8 issued — Sep 22, 2025.
  2. Hong Kong Marine Department / Terminal Notices: Suspension of port and terminal operations under typhoon signal conditions — Sep 22, 2025.
  3. Carrier advisories (multiple): Service disruption notices and port call adjustments related to Typhoon Ragasa — Sep 22–23, 2025.
Escalation Alert
December 2023

Red Sea security escalation triggers Cape diversions: extended transit times and schedule instability on Asia–Europe routings

Condition: Critical
Coverage: All clients with Asia–Europe routings, Med transshipment exposure, or time-sensitive ocean freight impacted by Suez/Red Sea route changes
Impact: COST: HIGH • TIMING: HIGH • EXECUTION: HIGH
Major carriers began diverting vessels away from the Red Sea and routing via the Cape of Good Hope in response to elevated maritime security risk. Operational impact: longer voyage times, unstable weekly schedules, equipment imbalance, and increased rollover risk as networks absorb added sailing days.
▾
Alert issued

December 27, 2023: Alert distributed after multiple carriers confirmed Cape diversions and warned of longer transit times and knock-on disruption.

Assessed impact

Cost: Higher ocean rates and surcharges likely as carriers absorb longer routes; working-capital carry increases due to longer in-transit inventory.

Timing: Treat ETAs as ranges. Cape routing adds days to weeks depending on loop and port resequencing.

Execution: Elevated rollover risk and unstable departures as carriers revise pro-forma schedules and reposition equipment.

Decision window

Next 7–14 days: Network adjustments propagate through schedules and transshipment hubs.

Identify priority SKUs and lanes that cannot tolerate longer transit. Authorize service upgrades for those flows (premium ocean products, or air conversion for a narrow subset). Convert fixed delivery promises into buffered ETA ranges and adjust replenishment planning for extended lead times.

Watchpoints

Carrier posture: continued Cape routing, blank sailings, and revised pro-forma schedules by loop.

Network constraints: congestion at Med transshipment hubs and equipment imbalance impacting load acceptance and rollovers.

Authority guidance: MARAD / UKMTO updates for risk posture and incident reporting.

Primary sources
  1. Maersk: Operations update — pause/avoidance of Red Sea / Gulf of Aden — Dec 15, 2023.
  2. MSC: Customer advisory — diversion via Cape of Good Hope; transit time increase — Dec 27, 2023.
  3. Maersk: Red Sea / Gulf of Aden situation — vessel contingency tracker (diverted vessels) — Dec 22, 2023.
Authority context (follow-up)
  1. MARAD: Maritime Advisory 2024-001 — Southern Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb risk — Feb 2, 2024.
Standing Advisory (Monthly Monitoring Module)
February 2026

Mexico overland disruption and cargo security — tequila imports (standing advisory)

Condition: Elevated
Coverage: U.S. tequila importer — Mexico overland legs to U.S. border crossings (lane-dependent)
Impact: COST: MODERATE • TIMING: MODERATE • EXECUTION: MODERATE
This standing advisory is maintained as part of ongoing monitoring and referenced in monthly updates. Overland corridors can experience sudden disruption and elevated cargo theft risk, producing transit time volatility and carrier constraints for high-value spirits.
▾
Operational relevance

Timing: pickup/linehaul delays and missed border windows can cascade into late delivery and inventory gaps.

Cost: premium trucking/security requirements, detention risk from missed appointments, and tighter insurance constraints for spirits.

Execution: carrier refusals, reroutes, and changing security requirements can alter planning assumptions quickly.

Monitoring signals

Embassy security alerts: changes in on-the-ground security posture and corridor disruption indicators.

Border signals: unusual wait-time spikes at key commercial crossings.

Theft trends: corridor/state trend reporting used to update posture in monthly outlooks.

Escalation triggers (becomes an EA if…)

Sustained corridor disruption: verified blockades/unrest affecting primary lanes or crossings.

Posture shift: credible reporting of elevated theft risk affecting spirits/high-value cargo on relevant corridors.

Carrier constraint: capacity tightening or new security/insurance requirements that materially change cost or timing.

Sources
  1. U.S. Embassy Mexico: Security Alerts (official postings) .
  2. CBP: Border Wait Times (port-by-port) .
  3. Overhaul: Mexico cargo theft reporting (trend input) .
