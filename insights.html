Escalation Alert
22 February 2026

Supreme Court Strikes Down IEEPA Tariffs: Section 122 Replacement at 15% Effective February 24

Condition: Elevated
Coverage: All clients — U.S. imports (broad-based)
Impact: COST: HIGH • TIMING: LOW • EXECUTION: MODERATE
The administration replaced IEEPA tariffs with a 15% global tariff under Section 122, effective February 24 for 150 days. Section 301 and Section 232 tariffs remain in effect.
▾
Assessed impact

Cost: Most goods shift to 15% Section 122 plus applicable Section 301 duties; landed cost will likely require a SKU-level refresh.

Timing: No immediate clearance disruption expected, but guidance could tighten near Feb 24.

Execution: Exemptions may apply (USMCA goods, pharmaceuticals, critical minerals, certain electronics); unverified SKUs may be treated as dutiable pending confirmation.

Decision window

EOD February 23: Actions to consider before the February 24 effective date.

Before Feb 24, consider validating exemption status for in-scope SKUs, refreshing landed cost math using 15% Section 122 plus applicable Section 301 duties, and consulting trade counsel on potential IEEPA refund eligibility while compiling 2025 duty records by HTS code.

Watchpoints

CBP implementation: Look for CSMS messages, new documentation requirements, or port-level clearance friction tied to Section 122 starting Feb 24.

Refund path: Watch for Treasury/CBP instructions or CIT filings that define eligibility, procedure, and timing.

Sources
  1. Supreme Court: Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump (opinion PDF) — Feb 20, 2026.
  2. White House: Proclamation — Section 122 temporary import surcharge — effective Feb 24, 2026 (150 days).
  3. White House: Executive Order — ending certain tariff actions — IEEPA duties terminated.
  4. CBP: CSMS #67834313 — ending collection of IEEPA duties — ACE/HTS guidance.
  5. Counsel Note: WilmerHale client alert — practical implications.
Weekly Brief
2 July 2025

Singapore transshipment congestion: elevated berth delays

Condition: Elevated
Coverage: Asia routings via Singapore — transshipment connections and schedule reliability
Impact: COST: LOW • TIMING: HIGH • EXECUTION: MODERATE
Carrier guidance flagged a sharp congestion deterioration at Singapore beginning mid-June, with extended waiting time at anchorage and delayed berthing. The operational risk is missed transshipment connections and rolled containers rather than a single “closure” event.
▾
Assessed impact

Cost: Limited direct cost unless expedited moves are required; secondary costs show up as premium trucking and recovery fees when freight bunches on arrival.

Timing: Elevated risk of connection misses and rollovers that propagate delays across downstream ports.

Execution: Most visible effects are schedule revisions, omitted calls, and split deliveries as networks recover.

Decision window

Next 1–2 weeks: Treat affected sailings as ETA bands, not single dates, and pre-decide expedite triggers for top SKUs.

For shipments routing via Singapore, confirm whether your carrier is re-sequencing port calls or changing transshipment plans. For time-sensitive inventory, consider earlier drayage booking at destination to absorb bunching when freight releases.

Watchpoints

Carrier service updates: Pro-forma schedule revisions, omitted calls, and connection guidance for Singapore-bound transshipment.

Delay trend: Whether advisories describe improving vs worsening anchorage and berthing delays.

Downstream effects: Rolled cargo, late container availability, and bunching at destination terminals.

Sources
  1. Carrier advisory: OOCL Logistics — Singapore Port Congestion (Jul 2025) .
  2. Carrier market update: Maersk — Asia Pacific Market Update (Jul 2025) .
Weekly Brief
10 June 2025

Panama Canal: monthly operations posture (May 2025)

Condition: Normal
Coverage: All clients — Asia ↔ U.S. East/Gulf routing exposure and schedule reliability
Impact: COST: LOW • TIMING: MODERATE • EXECUTION: MODERATE
ACP’s May operations summary highlights the current operating posture, including maintenance activity and capacity-management conditions that can widen ETA bands. Risk is schedule variability and booking friction rather than a discrete “closure” event.
▾
Assessed impact

Cost: Limited direct cost unless booking changes force premium routing or service recovery; indirect costs show up in inventory carrying and downstream expedite moves.

Timing: Planning should assume broader variability for sailings that are canal-dependent, especially for tight distribution windows.

Execution: Most operational friction is around booking, slot availability, and recovery timing when schedules compress.

Decision window

Next 2 weeks: Treat canal-dependent ETAs as ranges and confirm booking posture on affected services.

For time-sensitive SKUs, consider pre-deciding reroute triggers (alt services or air for exceptions), and validate carrier guidance on slot/booking expectations and downstream cutoffs.

Watchpoints

ACP advisories: Any changes in booking/slot rules, maintenance windows, or operational notices that tighten transit planning.

Carrier guidance: Service-specific advisories describing ETA band widening, rolled connections, or recovery sequencing.

Downstream effects: Late container availability and bunching at destination terminals as schedules normalize.

Sources
  1. ACP advisory: ACP Advisory A-18-2025 — monthly operations summary (May 2025) (PDF) .
  2. ACP index: Advisory to Shipping list (official postings) .
Escalation Alert
8 January 2025

East & Gulf Coast gateways: labor contingency watch (January 15 deadline)

Condition: Elevated
Coverage: East Coast + Gulf ports — importers with time-sensitive inbound freight and tight drayage schedules
Impact: COST: MODERATE • TIMING: HIGH • EXECUTION: HIGH
Labor negotiations introduced short-run execution risk at East/Gulf gateways through the Jan 15 date. A tentative agreement reduced immediate shutdown risk, but carrier/terminal contingencies and ratification timing could still drive cutoff volatility and rolled freight.
▾
Assessed impact

Cost: Spot surcharges are possible if schedules destabilize, but the bigger cost exposure is detention/demurrage and premium drayage when gates and appointments tighten.

Timing: Highest risk is cascading delays from vessel bunching, missed cutoffs, and rolled containers even without a full shutdown.

Execution: Terminal rules and carrier contingencies can change quickly (receiving windows, diversion policies, last receiving dates). Drayage capacity and chassis availability are typical pinch points.

Decision window

Next 7–10 days: Treat as a short runway to reduce exposure ahead of the January 15 deadline and ratification uncertainty.

Consider pre-booking drayage earlier than usual for high-priority containers, confirming terminal appointment availability, and validating carrier contingency plans (blank sailings, diversions, and last receiving dates) for the specific services you rely on.

Watchpoints

Labor parties: Ratification status and any implementation notes that affect operating rules at terminals.

Carrier/terminal advisories: Cutoff changes, receiving windows, diversions, and “last receiving date” notices for the specific services and terminals you use.

Operational signals: Yard density language, appointment backlogs, and rail dwell increases tied to East/Gulf gateways.

Sources

  2. ILA: Joint statement regarding master contract (deadline context) .
  3. Carrier market update: Maersk — North America market update (January 2025) .
